### Project Summary
U1 Palette Fit takes a multicolour 3MF from MakerWorld, Printables or a friend and the spools you actually own, and tells you which spool goes in which U1 slot. It runs in the browser (nothing to install, nothing leaves your computer) or as a one-line CLI:

```
https://designmaster-solutions.github.io/u1-palette-fit/      # drop the 3MF, paste your spools
node cli.mjs model.3mf spools.txt                              # same plan in the terminal
```

**Why.** A downloaded project carries the designer's palette, not yours. When you open it, the slicer shows those colours and asks you to map them to what is loaded, one by one: it knows the four filaments in the machine, not the shelf behind you. And the U1 has four slots, so a six-colour model needs a decision about which two colours can share a spool with the least visible difference. Both questions are colour-distance questions, and the standard for "how different do two colours look" is CIEDE2000 (ΔE00). Nobody should be eyeballing hex codes for that.

**What it does.**

1. Reads the palette from the 3MF (`Metadata/project_settings.config`, `filament_colour`) and which filaments the objects really use (`model_settings.config`; a painted model counts every filament).
2. Takes your spools as `name,#RRGGBB` lines, or a Spoolman export, and keeps them in the browser for next time.
3. If the model uses more than four colours, merges the closest pair by ΔE00 until four remain, and shows each merge with its cost (ΔE below 2 is hard to see; 2 to 5 is visible side by side; above 5 is clearly a different colour).
4. Assigns one distinct spool per slot minimising the total ΔE00 (exact search, not greedy), and prints the plan: model colour, your spool, ΔE, and whether you will notice.

**What the demo file says.** A synthetic six-colour project (`examples/six-colour-demo.3mf`, six filaments, five used by the parts, two reds that differ by ΔE00 1.05) against a shelf of seven spools (`examples/spools.txt`):

```
Palette: 6 colours, 5 used (source: project_settings)
  1  #FF0000  used (2 parts)
  2  #FA0505  used (1 part)
  3  #00FF00  used (1 part)
  4  #0000FF  unused
  5  #FFFFFF  used (1 part)
  6  #000000  used (1 part)

Merges (closest pairs folded to fit 4 slots):
  #FA0505 -> #FF0000  dE00 1.05 (hard to see)

Slot plan:
  T1  #FF0000 -> Bambu PLA Basic Red #FF0000  dE00 0.00 (hard to see)
  T2  #00FF00 -> Sunlu Lime #10F010           dE00 3.19 (visible side by side)
  T3  #FFFFFF -> eSUN Snow White #FAFAFA      dE00 1.00 (hard to see)
  T4  #000000 -> Polymaker Jet Black #101010  dE00 2.73 (visible side by side)
```

The unused blue is not given a slot, the two reds share one, and the lime is flagged as the swap you will notice. It sits next to Any2orca (topic 43858), which gets a MakerWorld or Prusa project into Snapmaker Orca's format and keeps every filament, even past four: Palette Fit answers the question that comes right after, which of your spools go in the four slots.

**Checks.** `node --test`: ΔE00 against the published Sharma 2005 reference pairs to 4 decimals, sRGB to Lab on known values, a synthetic six-colour 3MF built inside the test (six filaments, five used, one planted near-duplicate pair that must be the merge), distinct spools and the exact match picked when it exists, and a real Orca 3MF without `project_settings` (falls back to the `basematerials` colours).

**Boundary conditions.** It compares the nominal hex of the filament profile with the hex you typed for your spool; a measured swatch would be better and is not what most of us have. The paint tree of a painted model is not decoded, so a painted model counts all its filaments as used. No 3D preview: the plan is a table and swatches, which is what you need at the spool shelf. No physical U1 was used; the tool works on the project file, not on the printer.

**Next.** Read the loaded filaments from the printer (Moonraker) so the plan prefers what is already in the slots; ΔE per face area instead of per filament, so a colour used on a 2 mm detail can merge more cheaply than a whole body. Updates go in one comment below, as the Read First asks.
